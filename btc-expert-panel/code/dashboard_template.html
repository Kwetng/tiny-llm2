Who sits on the panel

  • Random Forest and Tabular Transformer: the two model families from the quant research project (projectskf), retrained here to classify next-week direction from 12 market and on-chain inputs.
  • Neuroplastic World Model: the V5 predictive-latent ensemble (neuroplastic-financial-world-model). Six networks learn to predict the next state of the Bitcoin "world" and next week's return; it trades only when 4 of 6 agree.
  • Jev (System One): TypeSafe AI's decision model answers "Will Bitcoin be higher in 7 days?" and names the market regime. In this run it is an offline stand-in, not Jev: a fixed trend-and-valuation rule.
  • Mini LLM tape reader: the from-scratch GPT in tiny-llm2, trained on daily returns written as letters (a = big fall … g = big rise). It writes 256 possible next weeks and reports how many end higher.
  • Phantom Flow: an open re-implementation of the paid TradingView indicator's three published modules on daily closes: an ATR trend shift (ATR 10 × 3), swing structure (breaks of 5-day swing highs and lows) and an oscillator (distance from the 21-day average in ATRs). Its "combo" call needs shift and oscillator to agree and structure not to disagree. It needs no training, so it is not refitted each year.
  • Chart JEPA: the technical-analysis seat. A joint embedding predictive architecture — LeCun's idea — renders the last 52 weeks as a picture and learns to predict the embedding of the next piece of chart rather than its pixels, because pixels are mostly noise. A logistic probe turns that predicted embedding into a probability. The window is scaled to its own high and low, so the model sees shape, never price level.
  • Fundamentals: the only seat that never looks at price action. It reads four on-chain measures — price against what holders paid (MVRV), miner revenue against normal (Puell), the hash-rate trend, and price against adoption (Metcalfe) — each scored against its own previous four years. Fundamentals are slow, so it changes its mind rarely and is expected to be weak week to week.
  • Market consensus: not a model. Polymarket runs Bitcoin contracts as a ladder of "will it reach $X" bets, so the prices are a picture of the range the crowd expects. The signal is the gap between what a +10% move costs and what a −10% move costs. It abstains whenever no liquid market existed, which is most of 2020–2024, because these markets did not exist then.
  • Panel chair: LONG or SHORT when at least 3 experts agree and they outnumber the other side. The two dashed grey lines are the earlier chairs — 3 of 5 (no Phantom Flow) and 3 of 6 (no market) — kept to show what each new expert adds.

Read this before trusting the numbers

  • Every model is retrained each January on data before that year, then used unchanged for the year. Nothing sees the future, but this is still one historical path of about 330 weeks.
  • 2020–2021 was a strong bull market, so buy and hold is hard to beat on return. Compare drawdowns and Sharpe ratios too.
  • The mini LLM memorised the past tape: on new data its surprise (about 4.9 bits a day) was worse than blind guessing (2.8 bits), and its results faded after 2021.
  • The Jev figures come from the stand-in. Set an API key and re-run to test the real model.
  • Phantom Flow here is not the proprietary script, whose formulas are private: it follows the published description with standard formulas, uses closes only (no order blocks or fair value gaps) and cannot repaint, because a swing point only counts once the 5 days after it have closed.
  • Research on historical data, not investment advice.
